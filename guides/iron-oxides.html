Telling iron oxides apart

Iron oxides and oxyhydroxides are among the most common minerals studied with Mössbauer spectroscopy. At room temperature many of them look alike, but their hyperfine fields and how they change on cooling set them apart.

Tips for telling them apart

  • The isomer shift is not enough. All of these minerals are mainly Fe(III), with δ between about 0.3 and 0.5 mm/s at room temperature. The hyperfine field and its behaviour on cooling are more useful.
  • Hematite or goethite? At room temperature hematite's field (about 52 T) is much larger than goethite's (38 T or less). At 4.2 K their fields are closer, but hematite's ε is positive while goethite's stays negative.
  • Magnetite or maghemite? Magnetite has a second sextet with δ near 0.67 mm/s from the Fe(2.5+) site. Maghemite does not. Partly oxidised magnetite falls in between, and the ratio of the two sites shows how far it has oxidised.
  • A doublet at room temperature? It could be lepidocrocite, ferrihydrite or very small particles of goethite or hematite. Measure at 77 K and 4.2 K to tell them apart, as the guide on temperature explains.
  • Lower fields and broad lines often point to small particles or substitution, such as aluminium in goethite or hematite.
